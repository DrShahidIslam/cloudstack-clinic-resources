Dental Treatment Comparisons

Most dental decisions are a choice between two reasonable treatments, not between a right answer and a wrong one. These tables set the choices side by side, in plain words, so you can see what each option involves before you sit in the chair. They describe the treatments in general. Which one suits your tooth depends on its condition, which only an examination and an X-ray can show.

Root canal vs extraction and implant

This is the comparison patients ask about most: a badly infected or broken tooth can often either be saved with a root canal, or removed and later replaced with an implant. Saving your own tooth keeps your natural bite and is usually the simpler path when the tooth is strong enough. An implant is the stronger replacement when the tooth cannot be saved, but it is a longer process with more stages.

Saving the tooth vs removing and replacing it
QuestionRoot canal treatmentExtraction, then implant
Is the natural tooth kept?Yes. The inside of the tooth is cleaned and sealed, and the tooth stays in place, usually protected by a crown.No. The tooth is removed, and after healing a titanium post is placed in the jaw to carry a replacement crown.
What happens at the visits?One or two visits to clean, shape and fill the root canals, then a visit for the crown that protects the tooth.An extraction visit, a healing period, a surgical visit to place the implant, more healing, then visits for the final crown.
HealingMild soreness for a few days is common. Normal eating usually returns quickly, taking care until the crown is fitted.The gum heals over a few weeks after extraction. The implant then needs months to fuse with the bone before it can carry a crown.
Long-term careNormal brushing and flossing. The crown should be checked at routine visits, like any restoration.Normal cleaning plus care around the implant. Implants do not decay, but the gum around them still needs attention.
When is it the better choice?When the tooth has enough sound structure left and the roots are healthy enough to hold the treatment.When the tooth is cracked below the gum, too broken down to rebuild, or the root canal has already failed.
What drives the cost?Front tooth or molar, number of canals, level of infection, and whether a crown is needed. See how costs are quoted.The extraction, any bone grafting, the implant and abutment, and the final crown, spread over several stages.

Braces vs clear aligners

Fixed braces and clear aligners both move teeth with gentle, steady pressure. The difference is less about results and more about daily life: braces work all day without asking anything of you, while aligners can be taken out for eating and cleaning but only work if you actually wear them.

Fixed braces compared with removable aligners
QuestionFixed bracesClear aligners
VisibilityBrackets and a wire are visible on the front of the teeth, though ceramic options are less noticeable.Thin, clear trays that most people do not notice in normal conversation.
Can they be removed?No. They stay on from fitting to removal, which is also why they cannot be forgotten.Yes, for eating, drinking anything but water, and cleaning. They should be worn around 22 hours a day.
Discipline neededCare with hard and sticky foods, and careful cleaning around the brackets.High. Forgotten or part-time aligners slow treatment down and can stop the next tray fitting properly.
Suitable casesThe widest range, including crowded, rotated and complex bite problems, in teenagers and adults.Mild to moderate crowding and spacing, and many relapse cases, where the planned movements are predictable.
CleaningBrushing takes longer and needs small brushes or floss threaders around the wire.Teeth are cleaned normally once the trays are out; the trays themselves are rinsed and cleaned daily.
VisitsRegular adjustment visits so the wire can be changed and progress checked.Review visits to check the teeth are tracking the plan and to hand over the next sets of trays.

Crown materials: zirconia, E-max and metal-ceramic

A crown is a cap that rebuilds a damaged tooth. The three materials below are all in daily use; the choice is a balance between strength and appearance, and between back teeth, which take the chewing load, and front teeth, which are on show.

The three common crown materials compared
MaterialStrengthAppearanceUsually chosen for
ZirconiaThe strongest of the three. Handles heavy biting forces well, including in patients who grind their teeth.Tooth-coloured and natural, though the most solid types are slightly less translucent than real enamel.Back teeth and molars, root-treated teeth, and implant crowns where strength matters most.
E-max (lithium disilicate)Strong, but not as tough as zirconia under the heaviest loads.The most lifelike of the three, with the translucency of natural enamel. Often the first choice where looks lead.Front teeth, veneers and visible crowns where appearance is the priority.
Metal-ceramic (PFM)A metal base gives good strength, with porcelain layered over it for colour.Good, but the porcelain can chip in a way that shows the metal edge, and a dark line may appear at the gum over time.Long-used option for back teeth and bridges, often where budget is a deciding factor.

Your dentist will usually recommend a material once the tooth's position and your bite are known. See the clinic's page on crowns and bridges for how crowns are planned and fitted.

Dentures vs implants

When several teeth, or all of them, are missing, the choice is between removable dentures and implant-supported teeth. Dentures are quicker and simpler. Implant-supported teeth feel closer to natural teeth but need surgery, healing time and healthy bone, or grafting to build it.

Removable dentures compared with implant-supported teeth
QuestionDenturesImplant-supported teeth
Fixed or removable?Removable. They come out at night for cleaning and soaking; see the dentures care guide.Fixed in place, or clipped firmly onto implants. They are brushed like natural teeth.
Surgery needed?No surgery. Impressions are taken and the denture is made in the laboratory.Yes. Implants are placed surgically, then left to fuse with the bone before the teeth are fitted.
Effect on the jawboneThe bone under a denture slowly shrinks over the years, so fit loosens and relines are needed.Implants pass chewing forces into the bone, which helps keep the bone in place.
Eating and stabilitySoft and cut-up foods are easier, especially with a lower denture, which has less suction to hold it.Much firmer bite, closer to natural teeth, once healing is complete.
Time to finished teethUsually a matter of weeks from impressions to fitting.Months, because of healing stages. In suitable cases, same-day options shorten this; your dentist will say if you qualify.
Ongoing careDaily cleaning and soaking, periodic relines and eventual replacement as the mouth changes.Daily cleaning around the implants and regular professional checks, similar to natural teeth.

How to read these tables

A comparison table can only describe treatments in general. Your own decision turns on things no table can see: how much sound tooth is left, the state of the roots and bone on your X-ray, your bite, and your general health. That is why the clinic quotes and recommends after examination, and why the most useful thing to bring to an appointment is a list of questions. The first visit checklist includes the questions our patients have found most helpful, and the flagship guide's dental implants page shows how one of these decisions is worked through in a real case setting.
