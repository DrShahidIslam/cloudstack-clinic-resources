Aftercare Reference

Dental Aftercare Guides

Good treatment is only half the story. What you do in the first days afterwards decides how comfortably you heal and how long the work lasts. This page gathers the clinic's standard aftercare advice for four common treatments: root canal, tooth extraction, teeth whitening and new dentures. These notes are the general reference; the instructions your dentist gives you on the day always come first, because they are written for your case.

After a root canal

A root-treated tooth has been cleaned from the inside, and the tissues around the root need a few quiet days to settle. Some tenderness when you bite is normal at first, because the ligament that holds the tooth has been irritated. Until the permanent crown or filling is in place, the tooth is also weaker than it looks, so treat it gently.

Do

  • Eat soft foods for the first day or two, and chew on the other side.
  • Take any prescribed medicine exactly as directed, and finish antibiotics if given.
  • Brush and floss normally, cleaning gently around the treated tooth.
  • Return for the crown appointment. It protects the tooth from cracking.

Do not

  • Do not bite hard foods, ice or nuts on the treated tooth before it is crowned.
  • Do not ignore a temporary filling that feels high, loose or lost; call the clinic.
  • Do not stop prescribed medicine early because the pain has eased.

More on the treatment itself: the clinic's root canal treatment page.

After a tooth extraction

Healing after an extraction depends on one thing above all: the blood clot that forms in the socket. It is the foundation the new gum and bone grow over, and the first day's rules exist to protect it. A painful condition called dry socket follows when the clot is lost too early, which is why the do-not list matters as much as the do list.

Do

  • Bite gently on the gauze pad for the first half hour, and rest for the day.
  • Eat soft, cool foods once the numbness wears off, and drink plenty of water.
  • From the next day, rinse gently with warm salty water after meals.
  • Sleep with your head slightly raised on the first night.

Do not

  • Do not smoke, use a straw, or rinse hard for at least 24 to 48 hours; suction can dislodge the clot.
  • Do not poke the socket with your tongue or fingers to check it.
  • Do not take aspirin for pain unless your dentist advises it; it can encourage bleeding.

More on the treatment itself: the clinic's tooth extractions page.

After teeth whitening

Whitened enamel takes in colour more easily for the first day or two, so the days after whitening decide how even the result looks. Mild sensitivity to cold is common and normally settles within a few days. The clinic's page on teeth whitening explains the treatment; the notes below protect the result.

Do

  • Stick to light-coloured foods for 48 hours: rice, chicken, fish, pasta, bananas.
  • Use a sensitivity toothpaste if your teeth feel zingy with cold drinks.
  • Keep up normal brushing and flossing; whitening is no reason to pause cleaning.

Do not

  • Do not drink tea, coffee, cola or coloured soft drinks for the first 48 hours if you can avoid them.
  • Do not smoke in the first two days; tobacco stains freshly whitened enamel quickly.
  • Do not use extra whitening products at home on top of the clinic treatment.

With new dentures

New dentures feel strange before they feel normal. Your mouth produces extra saliva at first, speech needs a little practice, and sore spots are common as the denture settles; small adjustments at the clinic fix them, and you should not grind or bend the denture yourself. Start with soft foods cut into small pieces, chew slowly on both sides, and read aloud at home to retrain your tongue. The full routine, cleaning, soaking and fit over time, is in the library's dentures care guide, and the clinic's dentures page covers the treatment itself. For the healing side after implant placement, the Knowledge Centre's dental implant guide walks through the recovery stages.

When to call the clinic

Most recovery is uneventful. Call the clinic on +92 344 212 3913, or use the emergency dentist page for urgent problems, if you notice any of the following:

  • Pain that is getting worse after the second or third day instead of better.
  • Swelling that is increasing, or swelling with fever or a bad taste in the mouth.
  • Bleeding from an extraction socket that does not stop with gentle pressure.
  • A temporary filling or crown that comes out, leaving the tooth exposed.
  • A denture sore that does not ease, or a crack in the denture.
  • Numbness that has not worn off by the next day.
